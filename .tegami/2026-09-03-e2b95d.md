### `@layer`

Cascade layers work. `@layer` groups rules into named layers whose order you decide, and that order
is consulted before specificity — which is how a framework's `#id` rule can be made to lose to your
own `text` rule without an `!important` anywhere. All the declaration forms are here: a block, a
bare `@layer name;`, a list, an anonymous block, sub-layers by nesting or by dotted name, and layers
inside `@media` and `@supports` in either order.

The ordering is CSS Cascade 5's. A layer takes its place from where its name is first seen, so
reopening one adds to its original position; unlayered rules beat every layer; a later layer beats
an earlier one; a sub-layer sits inside its parent's slot and loses to the parent's own rules; and
`!important` reverses the first two.

Layers belong to the document rather than to one stylesheet, the way they do in a browser. An
`@layer theme, base, components, utilities;` statement in a sheet of its own fixes the order for
every sheet that follows, and a later sheet reopening a layer adds to it where it already is. The
order follows the sheets that are attached, so inserting or removing one settles it again. A context
has room for 30 distinct layer positions, sub-layers included; past that they share the last one.

**`revert-layer` works as well.** It rolls a declaration back to the value it would have had if its
own layer had never been declared, passing over the whole layer rather than only the declaration
that reverted it. An unlayered one has no layer to reach and rolls back past every rule of its
origin instead, which is what `revert` does. `revert` itself no longer throws under UIToolkit and
the editor, where there is nothing to read the previous value back from.

Existing stylesheets are unaffected: everything unlayered keeps its relative order exactly.

Tailwind v4's output opens with `@layer theme, base, components, utilities` and every rule it emits
lives inside one of those four, so until now none of them reached the styling layer at all.
