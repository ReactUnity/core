### View timelines and `animation-range`

`animation-timeline: view()` measures the animated element rather than the scroll position: progress
runs from the moment the element is about to appear at one edge of its scroll view to the moment it
has gone past the other. `view()` takes an axis and an inset in either order, and the inset — one or
two lengths, percentages or `auto` — shrinks the scroll view the element is watched in, which is how
an animation is made to finish before the element reaches the very edge. The container is always the
nearest ancestor that scrolls.

`view-timeline-name`, `view-timeline-axis`, `view-timeline-inset` and the `view-timeline` shorthand
name such a timeline, the way `scroll-timeline-name` names a scroll container's. A name is looked up
the same way for either kind, so `.card { view-timeline: --card }` lets anything inside the card
animate on the card's own passage rather than on its own.

`animation-range-start`, `animation-range-end` and the `animation-range` shorthand say which part of
a timeline the animation runs over. Each end is `normal`, a length or percentage into the whole
timeline, or one of the named ranges — `cover`, `contain`, `entry`, `exit`, `entry-crossing`,
`exit-crossing` — with an optional offset into it. A range name written on its own covers the whole
of that range, so `animation-range: entry` is `entry` to `entry`, while anything else leaves the end
at `normal`. The names describe where the element is, so on a scroll timeline each of them is the
whole scroll range and only the offsets narrow it. Like the timeline, the range is a sub-property
the `animation` shorthand resets.

`animation-fill-mode` still has nothing to fill on a scroll-driven animation: outside its range the
animation holds its first or its last keyframe rather than stepping aside.
