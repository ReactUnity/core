### `:has()`

`.card:has(> img)`, `label:has(+ input:checked)`, `.list:has(.item:hover)` and the rest of the relative selector list: descendant, child and both sibling combinators, a comma-separated list of branches, `:is()` inside, and `:not(:has())`. It weighs what its most specific branch weighs.

Matching walks forwards from the element -- the children, then theirs, or the siblings after it -- and stops at the first that fits, so a `:has(> .x)` costs one pass over the children and never touches the rest of the tree. A `:has()` is also evaluated last within its compound, after the tag, id, class and attribute parts that can rule the element out for nothing.

Styles follow the tree. An element that a `:has()` has been evaluated on is remembered as an anchor, and a change anywhere under it or after it -- a class, an id, a `data-*` prop, a state such as `:hover`, an element inserted or removed -- re-resolves the anchor and what follows it. Nothing else is re-resolved for a stylesheet that does not use `:has()`, and nothing more than that for one that does; in particular the whole tree is not restyled on every change.

Spaces inside a pseudo-class argument no longer break the selector apart, whichever function it is.
