### A minified `filter` is read as written

`filter: grayscale(1) brightness(0.85)` comes out of a CSS minifier as
`filter:grayscale()brightness(.85)`, and parsing that threw a `NullReferenceException` that unwound
through `StyleSheet.Parse` — so a single declaration cost the whole sheet and the app rendered
unstyled. That is what anyone building with Vite got, lightningcss being its default CSS minifier.

`filter` is split as a list of function calls now. A call ends at the `)` that closes it, whether or
not a space follows, and a nested `rgba()` inside a `drop-shadow()` does not end the call around it.
**An empty argument list is valid CSS** and means that function's own default, which is where the
minifier puts it: `grayscale()`, `invert()` and `sepia()` are the full effect, and `blur()`,
`brightness()`, `contrast()`, `saturate()`, `opacity()` and `hue-rotate()` are the identity. A call
naming a function ReactUnity does not have is skipped rather than failing the declaration.

`backdrop-filter` shares the parser and gets all of it.
