### A unitless `line-height` is a multiple of the font size

**`line-height: 1.5` is one and a half times the font size**, not one and a half pixels. Every
unitless number was read as pixels, which made a type scale unusable: Tailwind's `text-base` asks
for `line-height: calc(1.5 / 1)` and `leading-relaxed` for `1.625`, and both came out as under two
pixels of leading. A bare number now behaves the way `em` does — against the element's own font
size, and inherited as the number rather than as a length, so a child with larger text gets
proportionally more leading. Every other property still reads a bare number as pixels.

**An operand of `calc()` is a plain number**, even where the property gives a bare number a meaning
of its own: `line-height: calc(2 / 1.5)` is the ratio, and `leading-6`, which asks for
`calc(var(--spacing) * 6)`, is six times a length. That also fixes division in a calculation that
cannot be worked out until it renders — `calc(1.5em / 2)` was thrown away as a division by a value
with a unit, and is now three quarters of the font size.
