### A unitless `line-height` is a multiple of the font size

`line-height: 1.5` is one and a half times the font size, not one and a half pixels. A bare number
now behaves the way `em` does, and is inherited as the number rather than as a length, so a child
with larger text gets proportionally more leading. That is what made a type scale unusable:
Tailwind's `text-base` asks for `calc(1.5 / 1)` and `leading-relaxed` for `1.625`, and both came out
as under two pixels of leading. Every other property still reads a bare number as pixels.

An operand of `calc()` is a plain number, so `line-height: calc(2 / 1.5)` is the ratio and
`leading-6` is six times a length. That also fixes division in a calculation that cannot be worked
out until it renders: `calc(1.5em / 2)` is three quarters of the font size, where it used to be
thrown away.
