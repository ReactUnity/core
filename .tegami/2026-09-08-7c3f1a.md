### Scroll-driven animations

`animation-timeline: scroll()` points an animation at a scroll container instead of the clock, so
its keyframes advance as the container scrolls and rewind as it scrolls back. `scroll()` takes a
scroller and an axis in either order: `nearest` (the default) is the nearest ancestor that scrolls,
`self` the element itself, `root` the host, and `block`/`y` or `inline`/`x` the axis -- there are no
writing modes here, so `block` is always vertical.

A container can also name its timeline. `scroll-timeline-name: --feed` on the container and
`animation-timeline: --feed` on anything it contains, itself included, tie the two together, which
is how an element reads a scroller that is not its nearest one, or one of several by name.
`scroll-timeline-axis` picks the axis at the container, and the `scroll-timeline` shorthand sets
both. A name declared on something that does not scroll names no timeline.

Progress stands in for the clock, so `animation-duration`, `animation-delay` and
`animation-play-state` no longer say anything. The rest keep their meaning: the timing function
shapes progress the way it shapes time -- the default `ease` puts the middle of the scroll range
past the middle of the animation, so `linear` is what follows a scroll exactly -- the iteration
count divides the range into that many cycles, and the direction applies per cycle. A scroll-driven
animation never finishes, since scrolling back is always possible, and full progress holds the last
keyframe rather than wrapping to the first. An infinite count would divide a finite range, so it
counts as one.

A timeline with nothing to read is inactive, and an animation on an inactive timeline has no effect
at all, not even a fill: `animation-timeline: none`, a `scroll()` that finds no container, and a
name no ancestor declares all leave the element showing its unanimated style, which makes an
animation meant for a scroll view safe to declare outside one. A container with nothing to scroll
yet sits at the start of its range and holds the first keyframe.

The `animation` shorthand takes no timeline, so like every other sub-property it leaves out, it
resets one; set `animation-timeline` after it. `view()` timelines, `animation-range` and
`timeline-scope` are not implemented.
