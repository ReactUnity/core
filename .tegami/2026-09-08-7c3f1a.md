### Scroll-driven animations

`animation-timeline: scroll()` points an animation at a scroll container instead of the clock, so
its keyframes advance as the container scrolls and rewind as it scrolls back. `scroll()` takes a
scroller and an axis in either order: `nearest` (the default) is the nearest ancestor that scrolls,
`self` the element itself, `root` the host, and `block`/`y` or `inline`/`x` the axis.

A container can also name its timeline. `scroll-timeline-name: --feed` on the container and
`animation-timeline: --feed` on anything it contains tie the two together, which is how an element
reads a scroller that is not its nearest one. `scroll-timeline-axis` picks the axis at the
container, and the `scroll-timeline` shorthand sets both. A name declared on something that does not
scroll names no timeline.

Progress stands in for the clock, so `animation-duration`, `animation-delay` and
`animation-play-state` no longer say anything. The rest keep their meaning: the timing function
shapes progress the way it shapes time — so `linear` is what follows a scroll exactly — the
iteration count divides the range into that many cycles, and the direction applies per cycle. A
scroll-driven animation never finishes, and full progress holds the last keyframe rather than
wrapping to the first. An infinite count counts as one.

A timeline with nothing to read is inactive, and an animation on an inactive timeline has no effect
at all, not even a fill — which makes an animation meant for a scroll view safe to declare outside
one. A container with nothing to scroll yet holds the first keyframe.

The `animation` shorthand takes no timeline, so like every other sub-property it leaves out, it
resets one; set `animation-timeline` after it.
