### `oklch()`, `oklab()`, `lch()`, `lab()`, `color-mix()` and `@supports`

**The four Lab-family colour functions** join `rgb()` and `hsl()` wherever a colour is accepted,
gradient stops included. All take the modern space-separated syntax with an optional `/ alpha`, and
the comma form too. Every component is a number or a percentage, a `none` component resolves to
zero, and hue is any angle unit. What `100%` means differs by function, because each space has its
own ranges: lightness is 0–1 in the OK pair and 0–100 in the CIE pair.

A colour outside the sRGB gamut is clamped per channel, which shifts hue on a very saturated
colour, so treat a wide-gamut colour as approximate.

**`color-mix()`** mixes two colours in `srgb`, `srgb-linear`, `hsl`, `lab`, `lch`, `oklab` or
`oklch`. Both percentages are optional and may sit on either side of their colour, and a pair
summing under 100% scales the result's alpha by that sum. Alpha is premultiplied, so mixing with
`transparent` fades rather than darkening. In the three polar spaces the hue may carry `shorter`,
`longer`, `increasing` or `decreasing`. A space this subset does not have, `display-p3` and
`rec2020` among them, makes the declaration invalid rather than silently mixing in the wrong one.

The colours stay lazy in both functions, so `var()` and `currentColor` work as inputs. The
percentages have to be literal.

**`@supports` blocks are no longer dropped.** They were parsed and then ignored, so every rule
inside one was lost. The condition is now evaluated — `and`, `or`, `not`, nesting, and declarations
tested against ReactUnity's own properties and converters rather than the web's, which is the point:
the web's answer would call `color-mix()` unsupported and `float` supported, both wrong here.
Feature queries that are not declarations, `selector()` for one, evaluate to false. `@supports` and
`@media` now nest inside each other in either order, and a `@media` inside a `@media` matches only
when both conditions do.

This is what Tailwind v4's output is made of — the palette is `oklch()` throughout, every opacity
utility is a `color-mix()`, and each refined variant is guarded on an `@supports` that now answers
true — so all of it resolves. Transitions and animations still interpolate colours in sRGB.
